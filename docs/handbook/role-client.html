Roles · Explanation

Client node

Your phone or handheld: sends and receives, never forwards.

TL;DR

What: A client node is a Må:rse device that sends and receives its own messages and never forwards anything for others. Your phone with a radio, a handheld, or the Android app's own node is normally a client.

Why: Forwarding costs battery and airtime. A phone comes and goes, so nobody can depend on it, and it should not spend your battery on strangers' traffic.

Strengths

  • Quiet: it transmits only what you send, plus a small hello and an announce now and then
  • Everything a user does works: messages, channels, files, voice, pages, alerts
  • Receives mail that waited for you in a post office
  • Cheapest role in battery and airtime

Good to know

  • Cannot extend coverage for anyone
  • In a group with no repeater, clients alone got about 95 % through in the simulated forest, against 99.7 % with auto
  • Make a handheld auto if it should help out when no repeater is near

bench-tested on Heltec V4 and XIAO nRF52840. simulated for group results.

What a client is

It is a full node with its own identity and address. It is the role most people use. It talks to other nodes through repeaters and, when there is a host nearby, through the internet backbone. It is called a client because it does not carry other people's traffic.

The Android app's own node is a client by default. When the app runs the whole host inside the phone, the phone is a Må:rse node on the backbone with its own identity, and still only a client on the radio side.

What a client can do

  • Send and receive encrypted direct messages with a delivered mark
  • Join channels (at most 4 on firmware nodes)
  • Send and receive pictures and files, up to 8 KB over LoRa
  • Send and receive voice messages and fetch maarse:// pages
  • Share an opt-in, rounded position and telemetry
  • Ask its home post office for mail, and drop mail there when the recipient is away
  • Keep up to 128 contacts in memory, with up to 32 pinned ones saved in flash

What a client leaves to others

  • Forward floods or routed packets for others
  • Act as a post office (radio nodes keep mail only for themselves, in 8 KB of flash)
  • Serve maarse:// pages (firmware nodes fetch pages but do not serve them)
  • Be relied on as part of someone else's route

What it costs in airtime

A client is the quietest role. After it hears a repeater it announces itself every 12 hours by default, and sends a small hello (22 bytes, never forwarded) at growing intervals up to 30 minutes when it has coverage. Before it hears a repeater it announces every 15 minutes, so a new device is found quickly. Everything else is traffic you create yourself.

Airtime of one 22-byte message over 3 hops

What your client causes when it sends one short message along a known route. Airtime computed with the project's formula at SF9, 125 kHz.

The data packet is forwarded three times and acknowledged three times. A flood of the same message would be repeated by every node in range.

Formula: maarse-core/src/airtime.rs (Semtech AN1200.13). Sizes from the airtime report, section 1.

When a client is the right choice

  • A phone or a handheld in or near a town. Repeaters will carry the traffic.
  • A device that is often off, in a pocket, or on a small battery.
  • You are not sure. A client never causes harm to the network.

Choose auto instead for a handheld you take into a forest or out of coverage with a group.
