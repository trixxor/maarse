Features · Explanation

Linked devices

One person, several devices, one account.

TL;DR

What: Linked devices let one person use several devices, a phone, a PC and a tablet, as one account. You link a new device to a master device with a QR code or an address and check words. Each device has its own address, and the others know they belong to you.

Why: People own more than one device. Without linking, each would be a different person: separate inboxes, separate contacts, and a message to your phone would never reach your PC.

Strengths

  • One account across devices
  • Linking takes a QR code or a pasted address and a comparison of check words
  • Channel messages from any of your devices appear as sent by you
  • Same words in the Android app and the web client

Good to know

  • A device is only as safe as the person holding it: a linked device is you
  • Channel messages are claimed senders, so someone with the key can make a message look like yours on your own devices
  • Linked devices are tested end to end on a PC but not flashed or hardware-tested

How to link

Linking a device

Press Play.

Technical guide, "Linking a device and the Settings menu".

On both the Android app and the web client the choices are in one place, Settings, with the same words. In the Android Settings, How this phone connects chooses between this phone being the node, going through my radio only, using my home computer, or running a server on this phone. Role and regions under This node sets the role, and changing it asks first because the node restarts.

What linking means for trust

  • A device on your own signed device list, and not revoked, counts as you.
  • A device of another person is shown as that person once its certificate has been checked.
  • Use each device's own address when you administer a repeater, not the account's.

Details

The account keeps a signed list of its devices. A host that sees a message from a device checks that list and any certificate. Direct messages are authenticated and have no problem with this; channel messages carry only a claimed sender, which is why a key holder can make a message appear as yours (spec 6.4). A public view that shows the device instead of the account, with a layered verified display, is parked as a someday idea.
