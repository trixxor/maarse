Review · 14 November 2026 · Må:rse v0.5

Må:rse vs Meshtastic, MeshCore and Reticulum

The report goes through all Må:rse features, tests them with fresh runs and sets them against the three other systems. It explains what we have overlooked and what an internal panel voted for, both for fixed setups and for mobile use. Last comes a roadmap.

In short

Where Må:rse is best

Routing, global reach and encryption. In simulation Må:rse delivers more messages than flood with 1/5 of the airtime. It is the only one of the four systems that both runs on the chip and reaches out over the internet without a central server.

Where we lose

Users, apps (no iOS app), maps and GPS, a power-saving mode for trackers and field testing. Range and power consumption have not been measured. Meshtastic and MeshCore have years of operation. We do not.

New findings today

In a dense net at SF12 only about 30 % of the messages are acknowledged. The history on the host is stored unencrypted on disk. The host stops when the window or session that started it closes. And the user interface confuses when a radio is plugged into USB.

Tests

191 tests pass, and none fail (cargo test --workspace). On top of that come 48 simulation runs, 2 runs of 2 hours with 1 % duty cycle and a new test of MeshCore ping.

Status 14 Nov 2026 (evening): since this report was written, the edge bridge is built into router, host and firmware; ESP32-S3 nodes have a Wi-Fi backbone (as a mesh node of their own, or as the host's LoRa modem over Wi-Fi, chosen in the Wi-Fi settings), with Bluetooth and Wi-Fi in one firmware and a long press on the button to switch; the display shows the active links and the IP; the host lists its devices (/api/devices) and uses TCP keepalive; and a boot loop right after flashing (a lora-phy bug) is fixed. All tested on a Heltec V4.3; see the roadmap for what is left.

Is Må:rse better? Yes on architecture: routing, security model, reach beyond LoRa and features per byte. No, not yet in practice, because "better" needs users, field measurements and an external review of the crypto. The road to "better on every point" is less about new features and more about measuring, hardening and making it easy.

What the tests show

  • Må:rse wins everywhere there are repeaters. It delivers more messages and uses 1/3 to 1/6 of the airtime. At 1 % duty cycle flood runs out of budget and drops about 300 packets in 2 hours. Må:rse drops one.
  • SF12 in a dense net is a weakness. Only 30 % of the messages are acknowledged, and channel messages reach 25 % of the nodes. With DEBUG_ACK=1 you can see that the replies to path requests die in collisions with other path requests, mostly at a margin of 0–5 dB, and that 1,715 transmissions were given up because the channel was busy. Flood is just as bad, but that is no excuse. We lack load control.
  • The mobile test is too narrow. One car through a fixed net is the easiest case for Må:rse. The hard case is a group moving without repeaters (hiking, rescue teams, sailing). Here Meshtastic's flood is strong, and Må:rse only has forest mode with at most 4 hops. The simulator cannot show that case yet.
  • No field measurements. All numbers are from the simulator, which uses log-distance without terrain. The only measurement from hardware is IP ping over LoRa at 1.45 s at SF9.

Lessons from today's work

  • The PC crashed with 0x3B SYSTEM_SERVICE_EXCEPTION while debugging with USB radios. That suggests the USB serial stack or the power supply to the boards is fragile. For fixed setups it is an argument for a Raspberry Pi or a powered USB hub.
  • The host stopped several times when the process that started it closed. For fixed setups the host must be able to run as a Windows service or a systemd unit.
  • "maarse-host has no radio of its own" confused, even though the radio was plugged into USB. The client should see connected radios and offer to connect to them or attach them.
  • A repeater was shown as "?", because MeshCore only sends the key in its first advert. That was fixed today. The lesson is that gateway logic must be tested against real frames and not only frames derived from the source code.

What we overlooked

From Meshtastic

  • Range test mode: automatic ping with a log of SNR and position. The cheapest tool for getting field data.
  • Power-saving mode for trackers and sensors (sleeps, wakes up to send).
  • GPS driver on boards with GPS (T-Beam; the WisMesh Tag has GPS!).
  • Canned messages and an SOS button, so the device can be used without a phone.
  • Maps in the app and on a shared map.

From MeshCore

  • Room servers (BBS): a shared board where you can read what was written while you were away. Our mailbox is only for one recipient at a time.
  • Sharing contacts as a link or QR (advert URL).
  • Automatic announce interval per role, which can be set from the app.
  • Configurable hash size for paths (1–3 bytes) against collisions in large nets.

From Reticulum

  • Network key (IFAC): a closed net where foreign packets are discarded before routing. Important for emergency services and companies.
  • Announce quotas per interface, so a busy channel does not drown.
  • More media: KISS/TNC, so RNode hardware and AX.25 can be used.
  • Daemon and service (rnsd) that runs without a user interface.

Our own gaps

  • The history is unencrypted on disk (history/log.txt).
  • No load control: see the SF12 test.
  • No simulation of a mobile group without repeaters.
  • The host cannot run as a service.
  • Acknowledgements do not cross the MeshCore bridge.
  • Only one identity per device, and linked devices are not built.

Scenario: Brønderslev → Fredericia by car

The base stands in Brønderslev, and the car drives 210 km along the E45 at 110 km/h (about 1 h 55 min). The base and the car write 70 messages in turn during the trip. The repeaters stand along the road. Run with the new flags --repeater-km, --repeater-gain, --base-start and --anchor-every in maarse-sim (seeds 1–2).

The range in the model matters. With "low" antennas (+6 dB per repeater) two repeaters reach each other at about 21 km at SF9, and the car reaches a repeater at about 14 km. With masts (+15 dB) it becomes about 87 km between repeaters and 40 km from the car at SF11. Reality depends on height and terrain, and only a field measurement can settle that.

How do the anchors work here?

Anchors are only needed when a route becomes longer than 12 hops. With 30 km between the repeaters the trip is only about 7 hops, so the anchors do nothing. With 15 km between low repeaters it becomes about 14 hops. Here anchors give +3 percentage points delivered, but cost about 2.5 s extra latency. So the anchors are not what decides the trip. Coverage is.

How do we ensure a connection all the way?

  1. Height beats numbers. Eight repeaters on masts every 30 km gave 100 % at SF9 with the lowest airtime. Low repeaters every 30 km gave 9 %: they do not hear each other. Put repeaters on masts, silos or wind turbines, or keep low repeaters less than about 20 km apart.
  2. LoRa cannot cover a 100 km gap. Even with masts and SF11 about 16 % is lost in the middle of the gap. The solution is not more hops but another way: the phone in the car has mobile data, and the app can already reach the home host over VPN (TCP). What is missing is doing it automatically. When there is no LoRa route, the node or the app must switch to TCP and switch back when a repeater is heard again.
  3. An outbox that waits for coverage. Today the car gives up on a message after 2 attempts (about 1 minute). The message should instead stay and be sent as soon as the car hears a repeater's announce. The base in Brønderslev already has the mailbox for the opposite direction.
  4. Choose SF by coverage. SF11 closes gaps in a net with low repeaters (96 %), but uses 10 times as much airtime. That is fine for a single car and bad in a busy net (see the SF12 test). A "travel preset" or adaptive SF per link would be better than one fixed SF for the whole net.
  5. Plan with the map. With GPS and a range test mode we can make a coverage map along the E45 and see the gaps before we set up repeaters.

Extra runs at 8 × 8 km: 20 people gave 57 % delivered. 18 people + 2 randomly placed low repeaters gave 70 %, and + 4 gave 62 %. SF11 gave 62 % (Må:rse) and 69 % (flood), but flood only acknowledged 51 %, and both used 4 times as much airtime.

What the forest test shows

  • Forest mode works. At 3–5 km relay delivers 99–100 %, on par with flood or better, with 25–45 % less airtime. Flood acknowledges worse at 5 km (91 %).
  • The client role is a trap in the forest. Without forwarding only 49 % arrives at 5 km. The client should automatically suggest relay when it hears no repeaters.
  • 4 hops are enough up to 5 km. At 8 km 7 hops gave only 3 percentage points more, so the limit is not the problem.
  • At 8 km the group is split into islands with about 4 neighbours each. All methods end up at 56–62 %, so it is not the routing that fails. There is no way through at the moment.
  • What can lift the 8 km case is "carry" (delay-tolerant delivery). Today the sender gives up after 2 attempts. But people move, and an island group often meets another within a few minutes. If relays hold the message and hand it over when they meet the recipient or someone closer, the movement itself can close the gaps. None of the other three systems can do that today.
  • One high repeater beats many low ones. Random low repeaters in the forest helped only a little (+3 to +11 percentage points). A repeater on a hill, in a car with a mast or on a drone would cover the whole area. That matches the finding from the E45 scenario.

"The one on the top": one person on a hill can reach a repeater

There are 20 people in the forest and one on a hill in the middle of the area (+10 dB for height and line of sight, half forest attenuation down towards the others). A repeater stands 12 km away with a base next to it. All 60 messages go between the base and a random person in the forest. Run with maarse-sim --hill, average of 3 seeds.

The finding: The person on the hill already forwards for the others if he is a relay, but flood suppression shuts him down. He hears the other relays send the same path request with good SNR, concludes that "the area is covered" (on_duplicate_flood in maarse-core/src/router.rs) and stays quiet. But he is the only one who reaches the repeater, so half of the routes are lost. The 4-hop limit and more antenna gain change nothing. If suppression is turned off for him alone, 100 % arrives, and even less airtime is used.

Proposal: bridge mode. A relay that directly (0 hops) hears a repeater or an anchor is a bridge and ignores flood suppression for 15 minutes. To know that the neighbour is a repeater, announces get a new role field (TLV, like position and name). It is backward compatible, because older nodes skip the field. The client shows "You are a bridge to the net", so the person on the top knows he should stay put. In addition, a client that hears no repeaters should suggest relay (see above), otherwise the one on the top becomes a dead end.

Large test of the bridge rule (1,000+ runs)

Six rules were tried in 17 scenarios with 10 seeds each. The scenarios are: hill at 3, 5 and 8 km with 10, 20 and 40 people, forest attenuation of 6 and 15 dB/km, hill gain of 0 and 20 dB, repeaters inside the forest (2–6 of them, up to 40 people) and a control without repeaters.   • The gain is robust. With the edge rule the hill is at 97–100 % (worst seed 88 %) in all 9 combinations of area and group size and at both forest attenuations, against 55–82 % today.
  • Physics still applies. Without height gain (+0 dB) the person on the hill does not reach the repeater at all, and all rules give 0 %. Bridge mode only helps when he can actually hear the repeater.
  • The naive solution is dangerous. "Bridge = turn off suppression when you hear a repeater" breaks dense nets with repeaters: acknowledgements drop to 59 %, and airtime quadruples. The rule must be limited to the edge of coverage.
  • The price with partial coverage: +27–32 % airtime and 1–3 points fewer acknowledgements. That is acceptable, but worth polishing, e.g. with a short wait only for edge bridges.
  • On hardware a node cannot "see" the link budget like the simulator. It must learn that it is at the edge: it hears a repeater (role field in announces), and it has neighbours whose floods it never hears a repeater repeat. That can be computed from the neighbour list, which already exists.
Implemented (update): the edge rule is now built into the router, host and firmware (nRF52840 and ESP32-S3), with no oracle. Each node announces its role and a flag saying whether it hears a repeater directly (app-data field 0x06). A relay is an edge bridge when it hears a repeater and has a neighbor that does not. As a bridge it ignores duplicate floods from non-repeaters, but a repeater's copy still silences it. Results with the real logic (10 seeds): hill scenarios 55–82 % → 96–100 % delivered with less airtime; dense repeater coverage nearly unchanged (98.0 vs 98.8 % acked); partial coverage costs up to ~30 % airtime and 1–3 points of acks, as the oracle did. maarse-sim --suppress normal is now the real rule and --suppress legacy the old behaviour.

The control without repeaters gives bit for bit the same result with all rules, and all 191 tests pass. The router has gained seen() (read only) and extra_flood_delay_ms (default 0). The simulator's knobs: --suppress normal|bridge|bridge-relay|bridge-weak|bridge-weak-relay|bridge-relay-late|bridge-edge|bridge-edge-relay|none, BRIDGE_SNR, BRIDGE_LATE_SLOTS.

Proposals, for and against, and internal vote

Five roles voted on each proposal: O operators (repeaters and gateways), M mobile user (hiker, emergency services), S security, N newcomer and D developer (maintenance and risk). The scale goes from +2 (must have) to −2 (do not). Above +5 the proposal is built, from +2 to +5 it is a maybe, and below +2 it is not built now.

Roadmap

Done since the report (14 Nov 2026)

  • Edge bridge in router, host and firmware (role field in announces with a "hears a repeater" flag).
  • Wi-Fi backbone on ESP32-S3: mesh node or LoRa modem for the host; Bluetooth and Wi-Fi in one firmware, button switches (3 s); crash guard; display with links and IP.
  • Host: device list (/api/devices, Devices in host settings), TCP keepalive on backbone links.
  • Fixes: stack overflow at boot (node in its own task, router in its own static), boot loop after flashing (lora-phy, firmware/vendor/lora-phy).
  • Simulator: forest, hill, highway with placed repeaters, anchors, suppression modes.
  • Partly covers automatic LoRa/TCP switching: a node can reach the host over Wi-Fi, but it does not switch by itself, and there is no outbox that waits for coverage.

Phase 1 · Now (0–4 weeks): measure and harden

  • Range test mode + first field measurement (Heltec and XIAO, SF7–SF12, town and country) → numbers into compare.md.
  • Power measurement on both boards (idle, receiving, repeater).
  • Encrypted history on disk (key from the access key or the OS keyring).
  • Host as a service: --service install on Windows and a systemd unit, with automatic restart and reconnection to serial.
  • Better radio choice in the client: see USB radios, suggest "connect directly" or "attach to the host (--serial)".

Phase 2 · Next (1–3 months): the air and mobile use

  • Load control: a quota on path requests by measured channel load, longer waits at high SF and an SF recommendation in the client. The goal is over 70 % acknowledged at SF12 in the simulator.
  • Automatic switching between LoRa and TCP for mobile nodes (the app or the phone over mobile data to the home host) + an outbox that waits for coverage instead of giving up after 2 attempts. The goal in the E45 scenario with a 100 km gap is over 99 % delivered.
  • Bridge mode Done (phase 1 candidate: small, measured effect): role field in announces, and a relay that hears a repeater directly is exempt from flood suppression. The hill test: 57 % → 100 %.
  • Forest and groups (the simulation is done: --forest): automatic relay when no repeaters are heard, and carry: relays hold messages and hand them over at the next encounter. The goal is over 85 % delivered for 18 people on 8 × 8 km (today about 60 %).
  • Power-saving mode on the nRF52840 for clients and trackers.
  • GPS driver (WisMesh Tag) + a simple map in the client with offline tiles from the host. The position is still private by default.
  • Network key (optional, à la IFAC) for closed nets.

Phase 3 · Later (3–6 months): services and ecosystem

  • Boards (rooms as in MeshCore) on the host and later on repeaters with flash.
  • The phone as its own node (maarse-host in the Android app, see the parked project).
  • Gateway to Reticulum (LXMF ↔ Må:rse DM).
  • External crypto review of the ratchet and the protocol, before v1.0.
  • Canned messages and SOS on devices with a screen and a button.

Maybe or not now

  • Updates over LoRa (shelved: too slow).
  • A native iOS app (iOS can use the host via the PWA, and an app costs a lot to maintain).
  • KISS/TNC and AX.25 (small audience, a lot of work).
  • Acknowledgements over the MeshCore bridge (needs changes on MeshCore's side).

Method and caveats

  • The simulator uses log-distance without terrain. The numbers are for comparing variants and are not absolute.
  • "Flood" is a model that resembles Meshtastic: everyone repeats, 7 hops. Meshtastic's real ROUTER_LATE logic and MeshCore's paths are not modelled, so the comparison with MeshCore is qualitative.
  • Information about the other systems is from compare.md v0.5 (sources fetched November 2026) and may be older than their latest releases.
  • The vote is a structured assessment from five viewpoints and not a user survey.
  • Repeat: cargo test --workspace --release, maarse-sim --seed N [--mode flood] [--sf 12] [--highway --car-kmh 50] [--duty 10 --duration 7200 --messages 240].
