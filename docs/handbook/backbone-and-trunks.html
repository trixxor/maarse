Backbone and trunks

How radio areas become one network over the internet and across water.

TL;DR

What: The backbone is how Må:rse hosts reach each other over the internet: TCP and UDP links, a lookup table (the DHT) that finds who can reach an address, and optional long-distance LoRa trunks as a backup. A radio area, a link and the internet are all just links to the router.

Why: LoRa reaches kilometres, not countries. The internet joins radio areas, and only the area where the recipient is hears the traffic, instead of every area repeating everything.

Strengths

  • No central server: the DHT lets any host find the right gateway
  • Same address and encryption over radio and internet
  • Per-link and per-address rate limits and bans protect a public host
  • An ESP32 board can join over Wi-Fi, a phone hotspot is enough
  • LoRa trunks give a backup path across water when the internet is down

Good to know

  • Needs at least one reachable host: a server or a host with an open port
  • Trunks are a backup: crossing the water with the internet down rose from 2.5 % to 11 to 15 % in the simulator, not to normal
  • A single operator with many networks is not stopped by the DHT limit of 4 contacts per network

Finding who can reach an address

The DHT is a lookup table spread across the hosts, Kademlia-like, over TCP only and never over LoRa. A host's ID is its Må:rse address, and distance is XOR. To reach someone far away, a host looks the recipient up in the DHT and sends straight to the host near them. A host that cannot open a port connects out to a server, which publishes the addresses it reaches through it.

Trunks: a backup path across water

A trunk is a LoRa radio with a directional antenna between two hosts. It uses the same frequency, spreading factor and settings as everyone else (decision 0003), so every node can always hear every other. It is a backup: routed packets cross only when the trunk is their next hop, alerts always, hellos and announces, and path or key requests only when TCP cannot reach the other end. Every 10 minutes each end sends a small summary of who is behind it, so requests cross only for nodes on the far side.

What a trunk gives

Percent of messages across the water that arrived.

With the internet up, trunks as a backup add about 9 points (39 to 48 %). With the internet down they take crossing from 2.5 % to 11 to 15 %.

Source: tools/sim-experiments/trunks.mjs, 2026-11-27, national preset, 30 % of messages to random users anywhere in the country.

How a public host protects itself

  • At most 4 DHT contacts from one network (IPv4 /24, IPv6 /48).
  • At most 8 incoming connections per address and 256 in all; 120 DHT requests a minute per address; each link rate limited to 100 packets a second.
  • A link that keeps going over the limit is closed and its address banned for 10 minutes; one sending more than 20 packets that do not decode, for 15 minutes. A block list can be edited in the dashboard.
  • A Wi-Fi modem must prove a shared passphrase, so that nobody who reaches the port can pass for the host's own radio.

Technical details

  • Frame: length (u16 little endian, 1..255) | packet. A zero length or more than 255 closes the link. TCP keepalive is 30 s idle then every 10 s. Outgoing peers are reconnected every 5 s.
  • First bytes of an incoming connection: D7 D7 a DHT request; 4D 4D a Wi-Fi modem; anything else is the length of the first backbone frame.
  • Region hello: right after connecting, each side may send its regions, and then only regional messages that match are sent.
  • Packets to one link: a packet with a fixed route goes only to the link of its next hop, learned from traffic; a retry within 10 s goes to every link.
  • DHT replicas for mail: the three highest rendezvous scores over the hosts in the DHT table; every host computes the same three for a recipient.
  • Finding seeds: --peer, --seed-dns (a DNS name with several servers behind it) or --public; contacts are saved in dht-contacts.txt. All of these are set in Settings in the web client.
  • Trunk summary: a Bloom filter of 1024 bits with 3 hashes of the nodes heard by radio on that side, about 2 % false positives at 100 addresses; a false positive only lets one request cross. Ignored when older than 30 minutes.
  • Wi-Fi modem key: a challenge-response with SHA-256, so the passphrase never crosses the network.
