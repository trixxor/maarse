Pages: browser and three designs

The browser on the PC host. Today the browser is locked to 860 px and the page to 720 px, so it sits as a narrow strip in the middle. Proposal: the toolbar runs across the whole top of the pane, and the page fills the rest. The page decides how it uses the room: a newspaper sets columns, teletext sets its 40×25 grid and scales it to fit, plain text stays plain. A page with no layout of its own keeps a comfortable reading width (about 75 characters), so a plain text page never becomes one endless line.

Today: toolbar and page in a narrow strip
Proposal: toolbar across the top, the page fills the pane (here a paper page)

Android keeps the toolbar at the top as today. Columns become one column (two on a tablet), side pictures go full width, teletext scales its grid to the screen width (40 columns always fit, it was made for small screens), and the paper look stays white.

A · The Harbour Gazette. A real newspaper: white paper, serif type, masthead with double rules, a lead headline over three columns, drop cap, a picture in a column, a pull quote, a weather box, and the notice board as classified ads in four narrow columns, with a line to place your own. On a phone the same page runs in one column.

B · Hals Havn BBS. An old dial-up BBS: black screen, the 16 ANSI colours, an 80×25 text screen in a mono font, ANSI art logo, double-line frames, a status bar with node and time, a main menu with hot keys in brackets, the message base as a numbered list, and a prompt at the bottom. Everything is made from @bg, @font mono, @grid 80x25 and hex colours in the .mu file. On a phone the 80 columns scale down to the width (small but whole, like a terminal), or the author can make the page 40 wide.

C · Pattern book. A reference people actually use: a contents list down the side (made from the headings by @toc), every feature as a card with the live result on top and the MU that makes it underneath, a copy button on every code block, the byte cost on each card, and the notice board where readers post their own tips. On a phone the contents become a row of chips and the cards stack.
