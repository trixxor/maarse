Roles · Explanation

Relay

The handheld that keeps a group connected when there are no repeaters.

TL;DR

What: A relay is a handheld that forwards for others, but only for a few hops and only when there is no repeater around. It is the "forest mode": a group of people on foot becomes its own small mesh. A handheld set to auto turns into a relay by itself when it stops hearing a repeater.

Why: In a forest or on a mountain there are no repeaters. Without relays, each person reaches only those they hear directly. With relays, the group reaches across the whole walk.

Strengths

  • No setup on the trail: auto switches after 2 minutes
  • Waits two slots before sending, so a real repeater always wins
  • Carries messages it cannot deliver and hands them on when people meet (carry)
  • A person on a hill becomes the group's edge bridge to the repeater network

Good to know

  • Uses battery: this is what the radio is for, but it is not free
  • Forwards for at most 4 hops, so a long chain of walkers is not a network
  • In a town, all-relay is harmful (72.8 % delivered, 18.7 % acknowledged): use auto, not a fixed relay role
  • Carry helps only when people actually meet

What a relay is

A relay behaves like a repeater with three differences. It forwards only while the packet has gone fewer than relay_hops hops (4 by default). It waits two slots longer before it sends, so if a real repeater is in range the repeater goes first and the relay stays quiet. And it carries what it cannot deliver.

What a relay does

  • Forward floods and routed packets for up to 4 hops
  • Wait longer than repeaters, so repeaters win
  • Carry a message it cannot deliver, with 4 copies for up to 30 minutes
  • Become the group's edge bridge when it hears a repeater and a neighbour does not

What a relay does not do

  • Forward beyond 4 hops
  • Keep mail like a post office
  • Split long paths (that is the anchor's job)

The edge bridge

A relay that can reach a repeater (someone who climbs a hill) is often the group's only way into the repeater network. A relay is an edge bridge when it hears a repeater and has a recent neighbour that does not. It ignores duplicate floods from other non-repeaters, so it stays useful instead of being silenced. Nodes without coverage pick the nearest bridge they hear and name it in their announce, and a bridge steps back two minutes after nobody names it.

simulated With 10 to 40 people on 3 to 8 km and one person on a hill that reaches a repeater 12 km away, delivery to the base rose from 55–82 % to 96–100 %, with less airtime (maarse-sim --hill, 10 seeds).

Carry: when nobody can hear anyone

If a relay cannot deliver a message, it keeps it and hands half of its copies to new people it meets, or to the recipient when it is heard. When the recipient gets a copy, it sends a tiny delivered notice and the carriers delete theirs. Read more under mailboxes and carry.

simulated Forest at 8 km: delivery 60 → 76 % at +9 % airtime. At 10 km: 30 → 44 % at +11 %. See simulation results.

How auto and the bridge decide

  • Auto: client while a repeater or anchor is in range; relay after 2 minutes with none; a repeater counts as in range for 5 minutes after it was last heard; back to client after 2 minutes of hearing one again. The node logs each switch and reports role 4.
  • Bridge selection: a node that hears a repeater while a neighbour without coverage has no bridge yet becomes a relay for it, after 2 minutes of steady coverage, with a chance of max(1 / (1 + neighbours that hear a repeater too), 1/4) per 15 s check. A person on a hill takes the job at once; nodes along a town's edge do not all do it.
  • Hello role flag: a flag bit says "I hear a repeater or anchor directly", so neighbours know who has coverage within seconds. Observations expire after 5 minutes.
  • Boundary forwarding: a bridge does not forward a path request from a covered neighbour, and forwards one from a repeater only when the destination is known to have no coverage.
  • Hop limit: 12 hops instead of 4 added about one point in the forest and nothing in the town, and with many relays it does harm, so 4 stays the default.

Cost note from the simulator: where repeaters stand inside a forest, the bridge rule costs 0–3 points of acknowledgements and up to about 30 % more airtime with partial coverage. With dense coverage it is close to unchanged.
