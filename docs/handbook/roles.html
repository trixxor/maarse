Roles and devices

Who forwards, who listens, and how to choose.

TL;DR

What: Every Må:rse device has one role that decides whether it forwards messages for other people: client (never), relay (a little, for a few hops), repeater (yes, always on) and anchor (yes, and it links long routes). On top of the radio roles, a host and a server are programs that connect radios to the internet backbone.

Why: A mesh only works if the right devices forward and the others stay quiet. If everybody forwards, the channel drowns. If nobody forwards, nobody reaches further than they can hear.

Strengths

  • A phone never drains its battery for strangers
  • A handheld can become a relay by itself when there are no repeaters
  • A fixed repeater turns one hilltop into kilometres of coverage
  • One role setting, changed in the app, no flags

Good to know

  • The right role is a choice: a client cannot help a group, a town full of relays cannot breathe
  • Roles are about forwarding, not about who may talk: every role can send and receive

simulated The charts on this page come from maarse-sim. Roles on real boards are bench-tested on two boards. Nothing is field-tested yet.

Who is who

  • Client nodeYour phone or handheld. Sends and receives, never forwards.
  • RelayA handheld that forwards for a few hops when there are no repeaters.
  • RepeaterThe fixed node on a roof or a hill that passes messages on.
  • AnchorA repeater that links long routes, normally one on the internet.
  • HostThe program on a PC or phone that joins radios to the backbone.
  • ServerA dedicated host for the internet: finds, relays and keeps mail.

The fifth setting: auto

Handhelds can be set to auto. A handheld in auto is a client while it hears a repeater or an anchor, and turns into a relay when it has heard none for 2 minutes. When it hears one again for 2 minutes it goes back to being a client. Nobody has to decide anything on the trail, and in a city, where repeaters are everywhere, the handheld stays quiet.

Energy changes what a node forwards

VoltageStateForwards
3.90 V or more, or USB powerfullEverything
3.60 to 3.90 VokEverything, a little later in floods (150 ms extra)
3.40 to 3.60 VlowOnly direct messages and path requests (800 ms extra)
below 3.40 VcriticalNothing

To go one step up, the voltage must be 50 mV above the threshold, so a node does not flip back and forth. The thresholds follow the Li-ion curve: 3.9 V is about 75 %, 3.6 V is the knee at about 10 to 15 %.

How auto decides

A handheld in auto becomes a relay when no repeater or anchor has been heard for 2 minutes. A repeater counts as in range for 5 minutes after it was last heard. After 2 minutes of hearing one again, it is a client again. The node logs each switch. Changing the role of a phone node asks first, because the node restarts.

An edge bridge is a relay that hears a repeater while a neighbour does not. It is the group's way into the repeater network, typically the person on the hill. See Relay.
